I have to say, this is a good show.

On the portrayal of raising children within a marriage — lately we always talk about having your own mind, being independent, not losing your value. But from the day two people enter a relationship, you have to understand: you give up part of yourself, part of your freedom, as the price of letting the other person into your life. With children it's even more so — they need far more of your time in companionship, guidance and education.

Of course people get tired, drained by endless trivialities. Lying, dodging responsibility, and dumping the burden on the other person is the most disappointing move of all. You could sit down together and work out a plan, carrying the responsibility on both your shoulders. Or you pay someone — or something else — to temporarily hand the responsibility to a third party, provided you understand the risks involved: trust, cost, information asymmetry.

Then there's the part where the detective takes a bribe. I've always believed that people test the line within their control — and sometimes cross it.

Everyone gets his price.

That price isn't necessarily money. Take the simplest example: crossing the road. At an intersection, you look around and there isn't a single car. You might not wait for that red light. Even if there are cars — you judge their distance and speed, figure there's enough time to cross, and you start thinking: do I really need to wait out this long red light? Even when the red light is only a few seconds from turning green.

But sometimes, even a hundred-second red light, you'll wait it out lawfully. So people are complicated — what mixes into the decision you make in that split second, what influences it, no one can say for sure.

Added on 2026/6/30:

Suddenly I have another reading. When the detective saw that his son's pants had been soiled all day without anyone noticing, and the teacher only said he "behaved very well" — it wasn't that he had no sense of justice. He chose to stake his career. He actively chose to give his son a better environment. He knew the consequences. He just made his choice.
