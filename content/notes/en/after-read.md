Today I watched the first episode of Chai Jing's documentary *Strangers: Conversations with Jihadists* on YouTube, in which she goes as a journalist to find the real triggers behind terrorists' attacks.

Why do I say "triggers"? Because I believe a person's actions — why they do what they do — always leave a trail you can follow.

One line in the video stuck with me:

"Terrorism survives on mystery. Expose it, and it loses all its control and collapses from the sky."

After watching, my take was this: a journalist — a good one — uses their professional ability to dissect a social issue, to understand and investigate the reasons behind it. That, I think, is what the profession of journalism should look like, and where its value lies.

Not chasing the speed of reporting or traffic with a one-sided narrative, or deliberately crafting sensational headlines that steer how readers judge.

It reminded me of a TED-Ed video I watched recently while learning English. Part of it recommended looking at things and opinions dialectically — for example, you should also understand why the opposite side has so many supporters: what draws them in, why they identify with that view.

So I searched Chai Jing on Bilibili to see if there were any dissenting voices. Most of what I found were clips and interpretations related to her old interview with Professor Ding.

I read a few, and scrolled through the comments.

The main line of attack was that the questions she asked in that interview carried a personal tendency to flatter the West.

Then I watched the original, unedited news program. I want to use one of the rare level-headed comments I saw among the flood to state my view:

"This isn't being brainwashed by the West — it's two sides clarifying a truth. If you don't ask that way, how could they explain it so you understand?"

To presume a person guilty — through micro-expressions, gestures, words, emotions — based on a single video, and then to escalate it all the way to personal attacks, I can't agree with that.

My thoughts here are only about the video and its comments that I saw, based on the information I have right now.

Finally, I feel that public opinion is something powerful — and also frightening. And I'm grateful that I grew up in a stable environment and reached an age of independent thinking before coming into contact with all kinds of voices in the world.

The online environment today really is restless, and people are all too easily led. I just think we should treat our lives more rationally.
