I think talking to AI is a lot like being trained to be a good leader.

You need to know exactly what you want to accomplish, and then articulate it clearly. Having been through it myself, I can tell you it's not as easy as it sounds — at least at first, you feel a bit lost.

There's the awkwardness of not being fluent in this form of communication yet, and also the growing feeling of gathering up your scattered, jumping thoughts, peeling them apart layer by layer, and slowly getting clearer.

This employee is amazing.

They execute exactly what you say. When you need them to push back and help you nail down your needs and direction, they're professional and thorough. And at the same time, they can observe your way of thinking and communicating from the conversation and distill it into lessons for you — so you grow and consolidate at the same time.

All you need to do is know your own limits, and communicate consistently and precisely. (And take responsibility for everything that comes with it, plus a bit of cost 💰 — the consequences include but aren't limited to the quality of the output, technical risk, legal compliance, and so on.)

When you deal with humans, you almost certainly won't meet an employee this "obedient." But once you've been trained to know what you actually want, express it precisely, and understand the boundary between your abilities and your employee's — maybe you'll turn out to be a pretty decent leader. (P.S. who doesn't like a capable leader who's also kind?
